# 0006 — Two Reconciliation Engines (Material vs Service)

- **Status:** **SUPERSEDED by ADR-0009** (collapsed to one engine — material reconciliation only)
- **Date:** 2026-05-09
- **Related:** [0004](./0004-attendance-driven-recurring-billing.md)

## Context

The PRD's "Financial Reconciliation" section (line 511–512) describes 3-way matching for materials only: **PO ↔ GRN ↔ Supplier Bill**. It says nothing about how recurring service contracts reconcile — and they have a fundamentally different shape.

A material order has discrete events: PO sent, goods received, bill submitted. A service contract has a continuous attendance stream that needs to roll up monthly. You cannot reconcile a 6-month guard contract against a "GRN" — there is no GRN. The reconciliation point is the monthly attendance roll-up.

## Decision

Two independent reconciliation engines:

### Engine 1 — Material Reconciliation (existing PRD design)

3-way match: **Purchase Order ↔ GRN ↔ Supplier Bill**. Mismatches surface in admin's reconciliation dashboard with line-item drill-down.

### Engine 2 — Service Reconciliation (new)

For each `service_contract` and each monthly billing period:

1. **Attendance roll-up** is the canonical source. Direct staff: `attendance_records`. Sub-contracted: `personnel_dispatches` from kiosk logs.
2. **Supplier bill expectation** is computed from the roll-up × supplier's per-day rate.
3. **Customer sale bill expectation** is computed from the roll-up × customer's per-day rate (per ADR 0004).
4. The supplier submits their actual bill. Engine 2 flags deltas:
   - Supplier bill > expectation → supplier is over-billing (surfaced to admin).
   - Supplier bill < expectation → supplier under-billed themselves (rare, log and accept).
5. Customer's invoice is **always generated by the Company from the roll-up** (it is not "submitted" by anyone), so there's no customer-side mismatch — only short-supply credits when applicable.

Both engines feed the same admin reconciliation dashboard with separate panels.

## Alternatives Considered

| Option | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| **One unified engine** | Material and service have different cardinalities, different sources, different mismatch rules. Forcing them through one pipeline obscures more than it clarifies. |
| **Service reconciliation only happens at contract end** | Surprise mismatches accumulating for 6 months are toxic. Monthly reconciliation catches drift early. |

## Consequences

**Good**
- Each engine has clear inputs and outputs.
- Service reconciliation surfaces supplier billing issues monthly, before they compound.
- Customer bills are uncontested by design (Company generates them; nothing to reconcile to).

**Bad / costly**
- Two engines, two UIs, two test surfaces.
- The service engine depends on attendance reliability; gaps in kiosk logs will surface as "the supplier worked but the system didn't see it" disputes.
